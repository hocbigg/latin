Hocbigg - Latin

Path to a free self-taught education in Latin!

Last updated on: 2026-10-01

Summary

Latin is the language of classical Roman literature, Roman law, and over a millennium of Western intellectual, theological, and scientific thought. Studying Latin offers unmediated access to foundational texts—from the political speeches of Cicero and the philosophy of Seneca to the epic vision of Vergil—allowing you to engage with Roman culture in its original register without relying on the inevitable interpretive choices of modern translators.

This curriculum is designed specifically for independent learners with no prior background in Latin or ancient languages. It takes an absolute beginner systematically to the level of reading unadapted Classical Latin prose and quantitative poetry with philological precision.

Curriculum Structure

Because Latin is a highly inflected synthetic language, mastery requires a cumulative, sequential progression. Each phase builds directly on the syntactic and lexical foundation established before it:

  • Pronunciation and Foundations: Begin with the restored classical pronunciation system to internalize vowel quantities and penultimate stress rules. Next, establish basic noun and verb morphology through either the natural inductive reading method (Lingua Latina per se Illustrata) or the traditional grammar-translation method (Wheelock's Latin).
  • Advanced Syntax and Bridging: Once core paradigms are mastered, tackle complex subordinate structures, the subjunctive mood, indirect statement, and ablative absolutes. Rather than moving straight from textbook paradigms into dense unadapted classical texts, work through intermediate transitional readers (Fabulae Faciles, Roma Aeterna) to build continuous reading fluency and vocabulary retention.
  • Unadapted Classical Prose: Transition into authentic Latin through late Republican historiography and oratory (Caesar and Cicero), whose periodic style forms the stylistic baseline of Classical Latinity. Progress from there to Silver Age Imperial prose (Pliny the Younger and Seneca), analyzing how rhetorical style shifted under the Principate.
  • Metrics and Augustan Poetry: Before reading classical verse, study quantitative meter and scansion. Poetic word order and aesthetic devices require deliberate preparation before tackling Augustan epic (Vergil's Aeneid), elegiac narrative (Ovid), and lyric strophes (Catullus and Horace).
  • Historical and Literary Frameworks: The historical and literary surveys in the final sections should be studied alongside or directly following your reading of authentic texts. Understanding Roman political institutions, rhetorical conventions, and literary genre expectations transforms translation from mechanical decoding into historical interpretation.

Beyond the Core

This curriculum covers only the essential linguistic and literary foundation expected of an undergraduate student in classical Latin. Once you have completed this sequence, you can branch out into specialized disciplines:

  • Explore subfields such as Roman comedy, historical linguistics, palaeography, textual criticism, and medieval or Neo-Latin in Advanced Topics.
  • Discover landmark scholarship, critical essays, and transformative monographs in Readings.
  • Deepen your auditory comprehension and cultural context through recorded lectures and seminars in Courses.

Curriculum

Classical Latin Pronunciation and Phonetics

Covers the phonological system of Golden Age Classical Latin, including vowel quantity, consonant articulation, diphthongs, syllabification, and the penultimate stress accent rule.

Vox Latina: A Guide to the Pronunciation of Classical Latin (W. Sidney Allen) - The standard academic manual on the reconstructed phonetics and orthography of Classical Latin; read for foundational linguistic principles and historical evidence.

Classical Latin Pronunciation Guide (polýMATHY / Luke Ranieri) - An accessible audio-visual tutorial demonstrating vowel length, diphthongs, and consonant sounds in restored Classical pronunciation; use alongside Allen for spoken modeling.

Latin Alphabet and Pronunciation (latintutorial / Benjamin Johnson) - An alternative, modular video series covering the mechanical rules of Latin consonants, vowels, and syllable stress.

Foundational Latin Grammar and Morphology

Covers the core inflectional systems of Classical Latin, including all five noun declensions, adjective agreement, pronoun paradigms, and the active and passive indicative and imperative verbal systems.

Getting Started on Classical Latin (The Open University) - A brief, zero-prerequisite online taster course introducing Latin cases, sentence structure, and word order; work through this module before committing to a full textbook.

Lingua Latina per se Illustrata, Pars I: Familia Romana (Hans H. Ørberg) - The primary inductive foundational textbook, teaching morphology and basic syntax entirely through contextual immersion in graded Latin prose.

A Companion to Familia Romana (Jeanne Marie Neumann) - Sequential grammar companion to Familia Romana; study chapter by chapter alongside Ørberg for explicit English explanations of inflectional forms, syntax rules, and vocabulary.

Familia Romana Audio Recordings (Luke Ranieri) - Complementary audio companion to Familia Romana; listen alongside each chapter to reinforce morphology and internalize correct macron quantities and spoken phrasing.

Wheelock's Latin, 7th Edition (Frederic M. Wheelock & Richard A. LaFleur) - An alternative, traditional grammar-translation textbook to Familia Romana for learners who prefer deductive paradigm tables, explicit rules, and English-to-Latin drill sentences.

Advanced Latin Syntax and Sentence Structure

Covers complex subordinate clause constructions, the subjunctive mood in independent and dependent clauses, indirect discourse, participial systems, ablative absolutes, and gerunds and gerundives.

Lingua Latina per se Illustrata, Pars I: Familia Romana, Chapters 23–35 (Hans H. Ørberg) - The reading-based continuation of foundational grammar; systematically introduces the subjunctive, gerundive, and indirect statement through continuous narrative Latin.

Bradley's Arnold Latin Prose Composition (ed. J. F. Mountford) - Sequential exercise manual to use after or alongside grammar study to master complex subordinate clauses and periodic sentence architecture through active translation.

Allen and Greenough’s New Latin Grammar (Dickinson College Commentaries / ed. Anne Mahoney) - A comprehensive reference syntax to consult alongside advanced reading for detailed rule codification and grammatical analysis.

LatinTutorial: 91 Rules of Latin Grammar (Benjamin Johnson) - An accessible video lecture alternative that visually explains individual subjunctive clauses, ablative absolutes, and syntax rules in concise lessons.

Transitional and Intermediate Prose Reading

Covers graded continuous prose narratives and accessible biographical history that bridge elementary grammar study and unadapted classical literature.

Ritchie’s Fabulae Faciles: Latin Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary (ed. Geoffrey Steadman) - The primary bridge reader; provides four progressive mythological narratives with running vocabulary and grammatical notes to consolidate syntax before tackling unadapted authors.

Lingua Latina per se Illustrata, Pars II: Roma Aeterna (Hans H. Ørberg) - The natural-method sequel to Familia Romana; provides extensive graded readings adapting Livy and Sallust, gradually transitioning the learner into unadapted Roman historiography.

Cornelius Nepos: Life of Hannibal (Dickinson College Commentaries / ed. Bret Mulligan) - An alternative or companion first unadapted prose text; features straightforward biographical narrative with interactive facing vocabulary and syntax commentary.

Republican Prose: Oratory and Historiography

Covers unadapted late Republican political and military historiography, forensic and deliberative oratory, and formal periodic sentence structures.

College Caesar: Latin Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary (ed. Geoffrey Steadman) - The foundational unadapted prose reader; presents military historiography from De Bello Gallico Books 1 and 4 with facing vocabulary and syntax commentary on every page.

Caesar's Gallic War (Dickinson College Commentaries / ed. Christopher Francese) - An alternative digital edition to Steadman's text, featuring interactive running vocabulary, grammatical links, and historical maps.

Cicero’s First Catilinarian: Latin Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary (ed. Geoffrey Steadman) - Builds sequentially on Caesar's narrative style; introduces formal political rhetoric, periodic sentences, and forensic argumentation with facing vocabulary and commentary.

Imperial Prose: Epistolography and Philosophy

Covers early imperial letter writing, Stoic moral philosophy, and Silver Age rhetorical style.

College Pliny and Vergil: Latin Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary (ed. Geoffrey Steadman) - A guided student edition containing Pliny the Younger's letters on the eruption of Vesuvius and early Christians, featuring facing vocabulary and notes ideal for transitioning into early Imperial prose.

Selected Letters of Seneca (ed. W. C. Summers) - Complementary reader focusing on Stoic moral philosophy, sententious argumentation, and Seneca's pointed rhetorical style.

Latin Metrics and Poetic Technique

Covers the principles of quantitative verse, scansion of dactylic hexameter, the elegiac couplet, lyric meters, and poetic word order devices.

Latin Metre: An Introduction (David S. Raven) - The canonical English-language textbook on quantitative verse, detailing the mechanics of hexameter, pentameter, and lyric meters.

Introducing Virgil’s Aeneid (The Open University) - A complementary conceptual course examining epic poetic structure, cultural themes, and heroic values before scanning original Latin verse.

Augustan Epic and Narrative Poetry

Covers epic narrative technique, mythological storytelling, and dactylic hexameter verse in the Augustan Golden Age.

College Vergil: Latin Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary (ed. Geoffrey Steadman) - The primary guided student reader for the Aeneid (core selections of Books 1, 4, and 6), featuring facing vocabulary and grammatical notes.

Vergil's Aeneid (Dickinson College Commentaries) - An alternative digital commentary to Steadman offering line-by-line notes, vocabulary aids, and audio recordings of the hexameter text.

Ovid: Amores, Book 1 (Dickinson College Commentaries / ed. Maureen B. Ryan & Caroline A. Perkins) - Complementary narrative verse in elegiac couplets; balances Virgilian high epic with witty mythological narrative and subjective erotic elegy.

Roman Lyric, Elegy, and Epigram

Covers personal lyric, subjective love elegy, and satirical epigram across diverse meters and informal registers.

The Student's Catullus, 4th Edition (ed. Daniel H. Garrison) - The canonical student edition of Catullus' poetry, featuring introductory essays, extensive commentary on lyric and hendecasyllabic meters, and running vocabulary.

Horace: Odes Book I (ed. Roland Mayer) - Builds sequentially on Catullan lyric; introduces Horace's polished Alcaic and Sapphic stanzas alongside commentary on Augustan poetic ideology.

Roman Historical and Cultural Context

Covers the political, military, institutional, and social history of Rome from the regal period through the late empire.

The Romans: From Village to Empire: A History of Rome from Earliest Times to the End of the Western Empire (Mary T. Boatwright et al.) - The standard undergraduate textbook history tracing Rome's political, military, and institutional evolution from the regal period through the late empire.

The Ancient World: Rome (MIT OpenCourseWare / Prof. William Broadhead) - Complementary university course offering lecture slides, timelines, and primary source readings illustrating Roman republican and imperial history.

Roman Literary History

Covers the chronological evolution of Latin literary genres, rhetorical traditions, aesthetic developments, and major authors from the early Republic to late antiquity.

Latin Literature: A History (Gian Biagio Conte) - The canonical comprehensive literary history; systematically analyzes genres, stylistic evolutions, and intellectual currents from early archaic drama through late antiquity.
